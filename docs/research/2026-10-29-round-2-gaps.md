## 1. Short version (for Julian)

**What changed in the plan after this round**

1. **Google does not publish whether most of Colophon's permissions are "sensitive".** Not on the scopes page, not on any API's own guide. Google shows the class only inside the Cloud console when a permission is added [G1, G2]. So the real review size can only be known on the day you add them there. What we *can* do is shrink the ask. The sign-in asks for 11 or 12 permissions. **Seven are the real core** (sign-in basics, Analytics, Search Console, Google Shopping). The other four extras (YouTube, Calendar, Business Profile, Ads) are the ones most likely to make the review slower and have the least value. **Recommendation: launch with the core only; add the extras in a second, later review.**
2. **Business Profile and Ads should leave the launch.** Business Profile needs its own access request, and Google asks for a business profile that has been verified and active for 60+ days [G12]. No lead time is published. And the code's Business Profile step only lists the press's accounts; it changes nothing, yet tells the press "your opening hours show on Google Maps" (Code, `google/connect.js`). That sentence is untrue today. Ads is simpler than feared (Google dropped developer tokens on 9 September 2026 [G16]) but the code still waits for a developer token that no longer exists, so the Ads tick box can never turn on.
3. **Two more Google Shopping faults, beyond the old version number.** Google says an account made through the API must be linked to a "provider" account, and that provider needs to be Colophon's own Merchant Center account [G10]. The code sends no such link. Colophon's own account must exist first, and it needs the domain.
4. **A shop that sits idle costs about US$1.35 to US$2.60 a month (about CA$1.90 to CA$3.60), mostly from ten scheduled jobs.** Against $19 to $39 a month, the margin stays about 80 to 88% after Stripe fees (section 6). The surprise is Cloud Scheduler: $0.10 per job per month, ten jobs per shop, and a *paused* job still counts [G28]. Two other costs can grow with success and are worth a guard before 50 shops: the bot check (reCAPTCHA) and website traffic past 10 GB a month per shop.
5. **Stripe Tax is real and covers Canada's GST, HST, PST, QST and Manitoba's RST, but it is a calculator, not a bookkeeper.** Stripe's own Canadian filing is only for businesses *outside* Canada [G22]. For Colophon, keep "plus GST/HST" at launch (row 15.9) and add a **threshold watch** for Quebec and BC. For presses' own shops, Stripe Tax is a later opt-in, not a replacement for today's presets.
6. **Domain Connect works only where each domain seller has accepted Colophon's template, one seller at a time, by email.** No lead time is published by anyone. Cloudflare and GoDaddy are on the list; several big Canadian sellers I looked for are not [G18]. Plan on the plain-words guide for everyone else.
7. **Things a platform like this commonly gets wrong, found by reading the sources:** Google sign-in tokens die in known ways and the code has no "please reconnect" path (section 8.1); YouTube allows only 100 searches a day for the whole project; budget alerts do not stop spending; Bank of Canada rules may apply to Colophon's payments role; Gmail has a daily send limit per press.

## 2. Google permissions: which are sensitive, and which can be cut

### 2.1 What Google publishes

- **Fact [G1]** (scopes page, updated 14 Sep 2026): lists each permission with its words, for example `analytics.edit` "Edit Google Analytics management entities", `webmasters` "View and manage Search Console data for your verified sites", `siteverification` "Manage the list of sites and domains you control", `content` "Manage your product listings and accounts for Google Shopping", `youtube.readonly` "View your YouTube account", `adwords` "See, edit, create, and delete your Google Ads accounts and data", `business.manage` "Manage your Business Profile on Google", `calendar.app.created` "Make secondary Google calendars, and see, create, change, and delete events on them". The page says sensitive permissions "are indicated in the Google Cloud Console" and "require review by Google". **It does not label any permission in the list.** It does not mention "restricted" at all.
- **Fact [G2]** (sensitive-scope verification page): "For more information about available scopes and their classifications, refer to the reference documentation of the API endpoints called by your app". Its examples of sensitive permissions are "reading events stored in Google Calendar, storing a new contact in Google Contacts, or deleting a YouTube video". It also says apps "must request scopes that require the least amount of access" and not a wider one "when a more narrow scope is available".
- **Fact [G2b]** (restricted-scope page): "The Verification OAuth API Verification FAQ contains the current list of Sensitive and Restricted scopes." **I read that FAQ (support.google.com/cloud/answer/13463817) and its text holds no list** (nothing for any of the nine permissions). The page may load the list another way. I could not see it.
- **Fact [G7, G8, G6, G4, G3]**: the Calendar, YouTube, Analytics Admin, Search Console and Site Verification guides each list their permissions with no class label.

**Where sources disagree.** Google's verification pages say the classes are in "the FAQ" and "the reference documentation". Neither holds them. The only place left is the console's data-access screen (Google Auth Platform), which I cannot open from here.

### 2.3 Cuts that shrink the review

- **Code** (`google/endpoints.js` `SCOPES`): 12 permissions in 10 parts. The core parts are `identity`, `analytics`, `searchConsole`, `merchantCenter` = 7 permissions (`openid`, `email`, `analytics.edit`, `analytics.manage.users`, `webmasters`, `siteverification`, `content`). `email` (`gmail.send`) is behind a switch. The five extras are `drive`, `youtube`, `calendar`, `businessProfile`, `ads`.
- **Recommendation, cut 1:** launch with the core 7 plus `gmail.send`. Move `youtube`, `calendar`, `business.manage` and `adwords` to a second review after the first press is live. Google's own advice is the same: ask for the least access [G2]. Each permission needs a written reason and the screen recording must show each in use [R1-F4], so every permission left out is a shorter video and fewer questions.
- **Recommendation, cut 2:** replace YouTube sign-in with a **pasted channel link** and Google's public data. Reason beyond review size: the default allowance is "100 search.list calls ... per day" for the whole project [G9]. Code calls `search` once per shop (`channelVideos`), so about 100 shops a day would hit the wall, and every shop's trailer list would then fail. **Inference:** the uploads list call is in the larger 10,000-unit daily bucket [G9], which makes it a better call. I did not read that call's page.
- **Recommendation, cut 3:** keep `drive.file` (not sensitive) and keep it behind its tick box.
- **Inference, cut 4:** `siteverification` could be swapped for `verify_only` only if the code stopped adding the shop's reporting account as owner. Google's Search Console API has **no user-management calls at all** (its index lists Search Analytics, Sitemaps, Sites, URL Inspection) [G4], so owner delegation through Site Verification is the only API route to give the shop read access. Keep the full permission.
- **Inference, cut 5:** swapping `analytics.manage.users` for `analytics.readonly` is possible only if reports were read with the press's own token on demand instead of the shop's reporting account. That is a redesign and ties reading to the press's refresh token living for months (section 8.1). It does not reduce the count (one for one). Not recommended for launch.
- **Different idea, same effect.** The shop's sign-up uses Firebase's Google sign-in for identity only (Code: `signup/`). Round 1 [R1-F5] says the 100-person cap applies to apps using sensitive or restricted permissions. **Inference:** identity-only sign-up is unaffected by the cap, so a press can always sign up and get a shop; only "Connect Google" waits for approval.

## 3. Business Profile and Google Ads

### 3.1 Business Profile

- **Fact [G12]** (updated 28 Aug 2026): to use the Business Profile APIs you must create a project, have an Organization account (made in the help centre), and **request access by contact form**. Requirements: "Manage a Google Business Profile that is verified and active for 60+ days. This GBP can be the applicant's own office or headquarters or it could belong to one of the clients they manage." Also "have a website representing the business listed on the GBP". The form must come from an email that is "an owner/manager" on that profile. Approval is visible when the project's quota shows **300 queries a minute** instead of 0 [G12, G14].
- **Fact:** no lead time is published on the pages read. A follow-up email is sent "after your request has been reviewed" [G12].
- **Fact [G13]** (policies, updated 28 Aug 2026): a software company that gives tools to end clients is allowed ("Software developers that manage business listings on behalf of end-clients"). Rules: tell an end client within **48 hours** of any change such as adding a manager; give them an easy way to stop and **7 business days** to let them disconnect; provide a demo account within **7 days** if Google asks; do not post review replies or edit listings automatically without the user's "prior specific and express consent"; do not cache or store the content beyond limited amounts [G13].
- **Code** (`google/connect.js`, `businessProfile`): the step only calls `listBusinessAccounts`, stores the account name and returns "Your opening hours and your shop's link show on Google Maps." **Nothing is written to Google.** The sentence the press reads is untrue.
- **Does it suit a platform acting for many shops?** Yes under the policy, with the duties above. But it adds a gated approval, a 60-day-old profile (Colophon is a software company; the profile would have to be a client's, and Julian's own Lyricalmyrical is off limits by `CLAUDE.md`), and a policy burden, for a step that does nothing yet.
- **Recommendation: drop it from launch.** Keep the off switch. Remove the Maps sentence until the step really writes something.

### 3.2 Google Ads

- **Fact [G16]** (updated 28 Oct 2026): "Developer tokens were sunset on September 9, 2026." You no longer need a developer token or a Google Ads manager account to start. Access is a level on the **Google Cloud project**. Enabling the API gives **Test** access (15,000 operations a day on test accounts). A token sent in headers "is optional and ignored".
- **Fact [G15]** (updated 28 Oct 2026): **Explorer** can be applied for in the console, "automated review and approval in most cases", **2,880 operations a day** on live accounts. Explorer cannot use account creation, user management, planning tools or billing and payment services. **Basic** (15,000 a day) needs brand verification first. **Standard** (unlimited) "involves a detailed manual audit"; the page shows "10 business days" beside the table. **Inference:** that is the review time for the higher levels. The page does not say so in as many words.
- **Fact [G17]**: Google Ads API versions now listed run **v23 (sunset February 2027), v24 (May 2027), v25 (August 2027)** and v26. The code's v18 is long gone. (Round 1 had this at a glance.)
- **Code:** `google/config.js` and `google/connect.js` show the Ads tick box only when `GOOGLE_ADS_DEVELOPER_TOKEN` is set; `googleApi.js` sends a `developer-token` header. **A token cannot be obtained any more**, so Ads can never switch on as written.
- **Inference:** the only Ads call Colophon makes is a product link (Merchant Center to Ads), which is not on Explorer's blocked list. Explorer's 2,880 operations a day would be enough for hundreds of shops (one or two calls per shop). So access is *easy*. The real cost is the `adwords` permission itself, whose consent line reads "See, edit, create, and delete your Google Ads accounts and data". That line scares a press, and reviewers will ask why a bookshop tool needs it.
- **Suits a platform acting for many shops?** Yes. Each press signs in and links its own Ads account; Colophon's project holds one access level.
- **Recommendation: drop Ads from launch too.** Fix the version (15.1) and the gate (new row) now, because both are quick and a real run needs them, but do not ask for `adwords` in the first review.

### 3.3 Should the extras be dropped?

**Yes, all four, from the first review.** Together they are four of twelve permissions, three need their own outside access (Business Profile, Ads, plus YouTube's tiny search allowance), and the owner-facing promise ("all in on Google") is carried by Analytics, Search Console, Shopping and email, which stay. They can come back one by one after the first press.

## 4. Domain Connect (plan row 6.15)

### 4.1 Who supports it

- **Fact [G18]** (domainconnect.org, copyright 2026): the home page says "1,200+ service providers and 300+ DNS providers". **The page that lists DNS providers shows only 11:** IONOS, Cloudflare, Domain Chief, FENO, Glauca Digital, GoDaddy, NameSilo, Plesk, Simply.com, Vercel and WordPress.com. **Where sources disagree:** 300+ on one page, 11 on the other. The list page may be partial. Treat it as "the confirmed list".
- **Fact:** I searched that list for the sellers a Canadian press commonly uses. **Found:** GoDaddy, Cloudflare. **Not found on the list:** Namecheap, Hover, Porkbun, Name.com, Gandi, and the registrar that took over Google Domains. Absence from a partial list is weak evidence, not proof.
- **Fact [G18]:** the Service Providers page lists 75, including Google Workspace and Resend. **Inference:** Resend already has a template for its sending records, which may help the email group (6.18), but I did not read it.

### 4.2 How a template gets accepted, and how long it takes

- **Fact [G18b]** (Getting Started): (1) write a template named `<providerId>.<serviceId>.json`, "as static as possible"; (2) fork the public templates repository and **send a pull request**, which the community reviews; (3) **"onboard with the different DNS Providers. At the moment, this is done by contacting them."** "Some DNS Providers may require contractual terms and further template review before publishing your template." (4) Sign the links; publish the public key. "It is highly recommended that people new to Domain Connect start with the synchronous flow."
- **Fact [G19]** (Cloudflare's docs, updated 23 Sep 2026): Cloudflare supports **only the synchronous flow**. `syncBlock` must be false; you must publish a `syncPubKeyDomain` TXT key; the link must be signed with `sig` as the last parameter; a template-check tool has a `-cloudflare` option. After your pull request is merged, **email the onboarding address on Cloudflare's Domain Connect page [G19]** with the template links, the key domain, an SVG logo, the default proxy setting for A and CNAME records and, optionally, a test account id; Cloudflare may first show it to that one account only.
- **Fact [G19], things that matter for Colophon's template:** Cloudflare ignores "TXT conflict matching", so a plain-text SPF line is *added beside* an existing one instead of merged (Cloudflare says to use the SPF-merge record type). Colophon's template puts SPF on the `send` host (Code: `TEMPLATE_GROUPS.email`), which a domain rarely already has, so the risk is small. A zone-apex alias record type is not supported. Redirect records are turned into redirect rules and *replace* existing ones.
- **Fact [G18c]** (GoDaddy's contact line on the DNS providers page): the contact line on that page (not copied here). GoDaddy's own write-up is from 2019; its line that service providers "were able to onboard in a matter of a couple of days using the examples" describes writing the template, not GoDaddy's review [G20].
- **Lead time: not published by anyone.** Domain Connect, Cloudflare and GoDaddy give none. **Inference:** plan for weeks per seller, not days, and for one seller replying while another does not.
- **Needs the domain first.** The template's public key must sit in a DNS record on a domain Colophon controls (G19). Another reason for the 5 Nov domain purchase.

### 4.3 Fallbacks

- **Cloudflare:** supported, and presses can also move their domain's DNS to it (the sellers list shows it is a DNS provider, not only a registrar). **Inference:** moving DNS is too much to ask of a press with a single book.
- **The registrar that took over Google Domains, and others not on the list:** no Domain Connect support is shown. **Plain-words DNS steps** (the existing 6.15 guide with Copy buttons) are the fallback. That guide must stay good, because it will be what most presses use at first.
- **Recommendation:** treat Domain Connect as an accelerator for GoDaddy and Cloudflare presses only, and measure how many presses actually take the one-click path before spending more on it.

## 5. Stripe Tax and plan row 15.9

### 5.1 What it costs and what it covers

- **Fact [G21]** (Stripe Canada pricing page, no date shown): **Tax Basic**: "0.5% per transaction, where you're registered to collect taxes" for Stripe's hosted flows (Billing, Checkout, Invoicing, Payment Links), or **CA$0.50 per transaction** (10 calculation calls included, then CA$0.05 each) through the payment APIs. **Tax Complete**: from **CA$120 a month**, a one-year contract, with registrations and filings (2 registrations, 200 transactions a month in the lowest tier). Filing partners are separate.
- **Fact [G22]** (Stripe docs, no date shown): Stripe calculates "federal tax (GST/HST) and provincial tax (PST, QST, or RST)": BC and Saskatchewan PST, Manitoba RST, Quebec QST. All product types are covered. Stripe collects only "in jurisdictions where the user has an active registration" [G23]. Thresholds listed: federal GST/HST **$30,000** over a rolling 12 months; QST **$30,000** over a rolling year by quarter; BC PST **$10,000** over a rolling 12 months.
- **Fact [G22]:** Stripe will **file** for you only if you are "an eligible non-resident business registered under Canada's simplified GST/HST regime". "This service doesn't cover other Canadian tax registrations." **Julian is a Canadian resident, so Stripe will not file for Colophon.** You (or the accountant) still file.

### 5.2 Colophon's own plan fee

- **Inference:** a Canadian seller of a digital service to Canadian presses is a normal GST/HST case; place of supply follows the press's province (round 1). Stripe Tax Basic would apply the right HST rate per province automatically, once you tell it your registration. Cost on the plan fee: 0.5% = **CA$0.145 on $29, $0.095 on $19, $0.195 on $39**.
- **Versus row 15.9** ("plus GST/HST" with province presets, no tax before registration): the row works for the harmonized and GST-only provinces. It **does not cover** Quebec's QST or BC's PST. Both start only when Colophon's sales to that province pass the thresholds above (Quebec $30,000, BC $10,000, per Stripe), which is far off with a handful of shops. **Whether SaaS is taxable under BC's PST is not established here; ask the accountant.**
- **Recommendation:** keep 15.9 as written for launch. Add a **threshold watch** to the control room: Colophon's taxable sales by province, rolling 12 months, with an amber line at 70% of the thresholds Stripe lists (this uses Stripe's figures; the accountant confirms). Re-decide on Stripe Tax Basic when the first province line turns amber. It saves a hand-built rate table, not the filing.

### 5.3 The presses' own shops

- **Code** (`apps/shop/src/app/admin/taxPresets.ts`): presets for printed books (5% GST everywhere), e-books (HST in full in HST provinces; GST + QST in Quebec) and general goods (GST/HST + PST/RST/QST). The comments cite CRA and provincial bulletins for the book rules. **The presets charge the listed rate to every address in a province, whether or not the press is registered there.** The press is told to check the rates.
- **Fact [G23]:** Stripe Tax by default charges only where the connected press has a registration, and for a SaaS platform "your connected accounts assume responsibility for collecting and remitting taxes". Each press must complete tax settings (head-office address, a default tax code, at least one registration) before Stripe Tax will calculate [G23].
- **Inference:** that registration gate is the real difference. A press that sells under the $30,000 federal line and has not registered should charge **no** GST at all. The preset wording does not say so. A press charging a tax it is not registered to collect is a tax-office problem, not only a rate problem. I did not read a CRA page on this today; it needs the accountant.
- **Fees:** the pages I read do not say who pays Stripe Tax's 0.5% when a platform turns it on for a connected account. **Unknown.**
- **Recommendation:** do not switch the presses to Stripe Tax at launch. Do add a plain first-screen question to the Taxes settings: "Are you registered for GST/HST?" with a "No: charge no tax" path, and a "registered in BC, Saskatchewan, Manitoba or Quebec?" prompt before any PST/RST/QST preset applies. Offer Stripe Tax as an opt-in later, for presses with several registrations.

### 6.5 What can break the margin (the growth side)

- **Website traffic.** Hosting gives 10 GB a month, then **$0.15 a GB** [G32]. A press whose shop moves 40 GB a month pays the platform $4.50 extra (30 x $0.15), and 200 GB is **$28.50**, the whole $29 plan. Large book photos and videos are what move the numbers. Storage downloads cost money too. Firebase lists $0.12 a GB for its older bucket type [G25]; Cloud Storage's own page covers the rest. **Inference:** a few busy presses can cost more than they pay. A cap or a traffic line in the control room matters before 50 shops, not after.
- **Bot check (reCAPTCHA).** App Check "creates an assessment ... each time your app refreshes its App Check token", **by default twice every hour**, and charges "above the no-cost quota" [G37b]. Free is 10,000 a month "per organization". The paid tiers read "10,001 - 100,000: $8.00 flat fee; more than 100,000: $1.00 per 1,000". **The page does not say whether the flat fee applies per project or per organization, nor whether the $1 rate covers every assessment or only those above 100,000.** Worked example (A: 4,000 sessions a month per shop, one assessment each): 10 shops = 40,000 = $8; 50 shops = 200,000 = **$108 to $200** (about $2 to $4 per shop); 200 shops = 800,000 = $708 to $800. At the high end that is 14% of a $29 plan. The default of "monitor" mode (`APP_CHECK_MODE`, `functions/CLAUDE.md`) still creates assessments in the browser. **Recommendation:** raise the token lifetime (Firebase allows 30 minutes to 7 days [G37b]) and set a budget alert on the organization.
- **Cloud Build on release day.** About 22 function builds a shop (my count: 17 in `index.js` plus 5 in `scheduledJobs.js`) at about 2 minutes each (A) = about 44 minutes a deploy. The 2,500 free minutes a month last for about 57 shops' deploys; past that, $0.006 a minute = $0.26 a shop per redeploy of all shops [G33]. A release that fails and re-runs doubles it. Small, but visible.
- **Image buildup.** Firebase's command line can clean images after each deploy, "by default" those older than **1 day** [G37]. **Inference:** the platform deploys shop code from its own service, not the Firebase command line, so no cleanup policy exists unless setup sets one. The high figure above assumes none.
- **Montréal storage has no free tier**, unlike the three US regions [G25]. Small today. It matters if photos grow.

### 6.6 What the cheap wins are

- **Merge the ten scheduled jobs into one "tick" job** that runs each sweep when due. Scheduler cost drops from $1.00 to $0.10 per shop (saves $0.90, about 3% of the $29 plan) and invocations fall by about two thirds if the tick runs every 5 minutes (8,640 instead of 24,784). Pausing a shop then needs one job, not ten.
- **Set an Artifact Registry cleanup policy in setup.** Saves up to $0.40 a shop.
- **Delete (not pause) the scheduler jobs for shops in "pause" and "closing".** Pausing does not save money [G28].

## 7. Money that can run away: budgets and caps

- **Fact [G40]** (Cloud Billing budgets, updated 26 Oct 2026): an alerts-only budget "doesn't automatically cap Google Cloud or Google Maps Platform usage or spending". **A newer "spend cap" budget exists, but only for four services: Gemini API, Vertex AI (now "Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform"), Cloud Run, and Cloud Run functions**; it must be scoped to **one project and one service**, **monthly only**, and reseller accounts are out of scope. Alerts come at 50%, 80% and 100%; the cap is enforced at 100% of estimated gross cost; costs are estimated faster than reports arrive.
- **Inference:** a spend cap on Cloud Run functions in each shop project would stop a loop or an abused public endpoint from running up the Functions bill. It would not stop Hosting traffic, Storage, Firestore or reCAPTCHA, which are the larger growth risks in 6.5. A cap that *blocks checkout on a busy day* is a risk to the press's sales too, so set it high (for example 20 times the idle figure) and make the cap's alert go to the control room, not only to email.
- **Recommendation:** every shop project gets (a) an alerts-only budget at a sane monthly figure, whose Pub/Sub message reaches the control room, and (b) a spend cap on Cloud Run functions set well above normal. The platform project gets the same.

## 8. Other things a platform like this commonly gets wrong

### 8.1 Google sign-in tokens die, and nothing tells the press

- **Fact [G38]** (OAuth guide, updated 28 Oct 2026): a refresh token "might stop working" when the user revokes access, when it "has not been used for six months", "the user changed passwords and the refresh token contains Gmail scopes", when a user exceeds "100 refresh tokens per Google Account per OAuth 2.0 client ID" (the **oldest is invalidated "without warning"**), when time-limited access expires, or when a Workspace admin restricts a service. A consent screen left in "Testing" for external users issues tokens that expire in **7 days**, unless only basic profile scopes are asked.
- **Code** (`google/tokens.js` `fresh`): when the refresh call fails, it throws. There is no "this press must reconnect" state, no notice to the press and no sign in the control room.
- **Inference:** the Gmail sending path is the most exposed: a press who changes their Google password silently loses order emails from their own address, and orders confirmations are the email that matters most. A press who is inactive for six months loses the Search Console and Analytics link without noticing.
- **Recommendation:** treat a refresh failure as a state ("Google needs you to reconnect"), show it in the shop admin and the control room, and have order emails fall back to Colophon's sender at once, not fail. Also keep test sign-ins on the separate test project (round 1) because "Testing" tokens last 7 days.

### 8.2 Gmail has a daily send limit per press

- **Fact [G39]** (Workspace help): up to **2,000 messages a day** per user account, 500 for trial accounts, "1,500 for mail merge"; after the limit the user "can't send new messages for up to 24 hours". Consumer help: more than **500 emails a day** or 500 recipients in one email triggers a block.
- **Inference:** order emails plus a press's own mail share that limit. A launch-day sale spike or a newsletter pushes it. Today's code has no count and no fallback.
- **Recommendation:** count sends per press per day; at 80% of 500 (consumer) or 2,000 (Workspace), route shop emails through the shop's own domain or Colophon's sender. The consumer-versus-Workspace choice is the press's account type, which the sign-in answer may show.

### 8.3 Is Colophon a "payment service provider" under Canada's retail payments law?

- **Fact [G41]** (Bank of Canada, "Case scenarios about e-commerce web building tools and payment gateways", published 21 Aug 2024; supervisory FAQ, Jan 2026): a company that sells a site-building tool and sends shoppers to a *different* company's checkout page "does not perform any payment functions" and is **not** a PSP. A company that captures payment details and relays them (a gateway) "meets the definition of a PSP" even though it does not hold funds, unless the activity is "incidental to another service". A web builder that *adds its own* payment gateway is a PSP "unless its payment services can be considered incidental". The same page says these are examples, "not legal advice".
- **Code:** Colophon's payment service creates the Stripe Checkout session on the press's account and takes an application fee (`services/payments`, `payAsYouSell.js`). The buyer pays on **Stripe's hosted page** (round 1; `apps/shop/functions/CLAUDE.md` mentions the embedded payment element too).
- **Inference:** the hosted-page route looks like Company A in the first example. The embedded payment element, where the shop's own page collects the card, may look more like a gateway. That difference could matter. The pay-as-you-sell fee adds a second angle. I did not find a Bank of Canada page that settles a *platform with Connect and application fees* directly.
- **Recommendation:** put the one question to the lawyer (section 1). Until answered, document in `docs/decisions.md` that Colophon never holds or sends funds and never sees a card number. Do not describe Colophon as "processing payments" in the terms, the plan page or the help.

### 8.4 Which countries presses can be in

- **Fact [G42]** (Stripe cross-border payouts): "Platforms based in the US, UK, EEA, Canada, or Switzerland can transfer funds to connected accounts in any of those regions".
- **Inference:** a US or UK press could be connected. The shop's tax presets, postal codes, Shippo labels and Canadian-dollar prices are Canadian. The pages for plan tax (15.9) only cover Canadian provinces.
- **Recommendation:** at launch, accept presses in **Canada only** (one sign-up question), and say "Canada first" on the waitlist. Re-open for the US when tax, address and shipping are ready.

### 8.5 Google Shopping: Colophon's own account must exist first

- **Fact [G10]** (Merchant API, updated 26 Oct 2026): creating an account through the API means `accounts.createAndConfigure`, and "you must link the new account to a provider by specifying at least one of accountAggregation, accountManagement, or comparisonShopping in the service field". For account management, you must also add at least one user. You "must have administrator access to the provider account". Account aggregation requires "an advanced account" that Google configures after "reach out to support". The page also says the Merchant API starts from "a Merchant Center account ... created ... using the Merchant Center user interface".
- **Code** (`google/googleApi.js` `createMerchantAccount`): sends the account name, time zone, language and users. **No `service` field and no provider.** Per the page above this call would be refused.
- **Fact [R1-F13]:** a one-time `registerGcp` call, also needs a live Merchant Center account with a verified home page.
- **Recommendation:** add the provider link to the call and make "Colophon's own Merchant Center account, with the home page verified and `registerGcp` done" a visible setup step in the "is Google ready" self-check (15.4), before any press is asked.

### 8.6 Smaller items

- **Auth and SMS.** Shops use Google sign-in and email links only (Code: `Account.tsx`, `Checkout.tsx`, `admin/api.ts`; no phone sign-in anywhere). **No SMS charges can arise today** [G35]. Keep it that way: a phone sign-in turned on later bills per text and attackers abuse it. One project per shop means Firebase Auth's multi-tenancy feature is not needed (Code: one project per press, `setup/`).
- **Stripe side.** For direct charges the press carries the Stripe fees, refunds and disputes, and Stripe covers loss for those accounts [G24]. With "Stripe handles pricing", Colophon pays no account, payout or tax-reporting fee [G24]. This matches the code. The extra fee line a platform pays (CA$2 per active account a month plus 0.25% + CA$0.25 per payout) applies only if Colophon sets its own pricing. **Keep Stripe handling pricing** unless the pay-as-you-sell fee needs it; the fee is an application fee on charges, which is allowed under "Stripe handles pricing" (Code: `payAsYouSell.js`; I did not find a Stripe page that says otherwise).
- **Platform billing, the shape matters.** Google Cloud's billing account holds all shop projects. **Inference:** one bad shop (abuse, a loop) lands on Colophon's bill. This is why section 7 matters before the 10th shop.
